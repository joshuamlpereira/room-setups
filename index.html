Room Setups

Floor plans of each way we can arrange Maple, Oak and Willow, drawn to scale. Pick the setup that fits your event and tell us its name when you book. Tap any floor plan to see it larger.

The rooms

Maple

2,027 sq ft. Curved wall of windows, TV, and a storage area past the TV wall that is not used for seating.

Oak

Full Oak is 2,250 sq ft. A moving wall splits it into Oak and the Double Meeting Room. A second wall splits the Double Meeting Room into Hickory and Sycamore.

Willow

805 sq ft. Carpet floor, counter with sink, and a TV on the long wall that people face. There is no whiteboard on the wall; a small rolling whiteboard is kept in storage.

The setups

The floor plans are drawn to scale from the building floor plan, with 6 ft long tables and 5 ft round tables. Table and chair counts stay within our capacity sheet. Treat them as a starting point and confirm the final count with us.

Banquet

Round tables with chairs all the way around. Each round seats up to 8. Food goes on 6 ft tables along the left wall in Maple, under the whiteboard in Oak, along the west wall in Full Oak, and on the counter in Willow.

Good for Dinners, receptions, award nights, group work.

Lecture

Rows of chairs facing the front. No tables.

Good for Talks, presentations, info sessions.

Panel

Rows of chairs facing a long head table for the speakers.

Good for Panel talks, Q and A sessions, hearings.

Job Fair

Long tables around the edge of the room with the middle left open for walking.

Good for Job fairs, vendor tables, check in, info fairs.

Classroom

Rows of long tables with chairs on one side, all facing the front, plus a table and chair for the teacher.

Good for Training, testing, note taking, workshops.

Conference

Long tables pushed together in a rectangle with chairs around the outside.

Good for Meetings where everyone needs to see each other.

U-Shape

Long tables in a U with chairs on the outside and a presenter table at the open end.

Good for Training and meetings with one presenter.

Horseshoe

Chairs in a curve facing a presenter table. No tables for the group.

Good for Small group talks and discussions.

Pod

Small groups of chairs spread around the room, with a presenter table at the front.

Good for Breakout groups and team activities.

Theatre Seating

Built in tiered seats that pull out from the wall. Oak only. Seats 150.

Good for Large talks, film showings, performances.

Boardroom

Long tables pushed together into one big table with chairs on all sides.

Good for Board meetings and small group discussions.

Cabaret

Round tables with chairs on the side away from the speaker, all turned toward one focal point: the speaker table. Food tables go along a side wall.

Good for Dinners with a speaker, trivia nights, award shows.

Exam

Single tables in rows with one chair each, spaced apart.

Good for Tests and quiet individual work.

New ideas

Extra arrangements we came up with using the same round tables, long tables and chairs. They are drawn to the same scale and stay within the same capacity limits.

Center Floor

Round tables set around an open floor in the middle, so every table has a view of it.

Good for Dances, performances, award nights, cultural shows.

Long Table Dinner

Long tables pushed end to end in long rows, with chairs on both sides.

Good for Family style meals, team dinners, potlucks, speed networking.

Workshop Stations

Islands made of two long tables, six chairs each, with a presenter table at the front. Groups can rotate from island to island.

Good for Hands on workshops, craft nights, training with breakout tasks.

What to tell us when you book

  • The room and the setup name from this page.
  • How many tables and chairs you need, and which kind of table (round or long).
  • If you need a TV or projector, speakers, a microphone or extension cords.
  • If you need time to set up before the event starts.
